\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Cough and respiratory audio screening.}
COVID-era cough audio studies motivated public datasets such as Coswara, COUGHVID, DiCOVA, COVID-19 Sounds, and the UK COVID-19 Vocal Audio Dataset \cite{orlandic2021coughvid,bhattacharya2023coswara,dicova2021,han2022sounds,budd2024ukcovid}. These datasets enabled rapid modeling, including recent cross-dataset COVID cough classifiers such as DNDT/DNDF \cite{saravanan2026dndt}. They also introduced concerns about label quality, recruitment bias, recording conditions, and external validity. In particular, audio-based COVID screening has been shown not to improve over simple symptom checkers under realistic analysis \cite{coppock2024nmi}. Our work follows this conservative view: cough audio experiments should not be over-interpreted as clinical diagnostic evidence without strong external validation and confound analysis.

\paragraph{Respiratory foundation models and cough representations.}
Recent work has moved from task-specific cough classifiers toward general respiratory audio representations. OPERA provides a respiratory acoustic foundation-model benchmark across many health tasks \cite{opera2024}. CoughViT studies self-supervised cough representation learning with masked spectrogram modeling \cite{luong2025coughvit}. Coughprint distills cough representations from speech foundation model embeddings for edge-compute applications \cite{coughprint2025}. These works show that respiratory and cough representation learning is a meaningful direction. Our focus is different: we audit whether teacher-to-student distillation remains reliable for ultra-light cough students when the target dataset shifts.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation under distribution shift.}
Response KD transfers softened teacher logits \cite{hinton2015distilling}, while feature and relation KD transfer intermediate representations or sample geometry \cite{romero2015fitnets,tian2020crd}. ShiftKD recently benchmarks KD under distribution shift in general settings and shows that KD behavior can vary substantially across shifts and methods \cite{shiftkd2025}. Respiratory sound KD has also been explored with architecture-agnostic ensemble distillation \cite{toikkanen2025respiratorykd}. We therefore do not claim to be first to study KD under shift or KD for respiratory audio. Our contribution is a domain-specific deployment audit for cough audio that combines cross-dataset transfer, calibration, domain/task probes, slice-level negative transfer, bootstrap target-composition sensitivity, clip-vs-subject aggregation, and efficiency metrics in one protocol.

\paragraph{Dataset and failure cartography.}
Dataset cartography and failure auditing approaches argue that aggregate metrics can hide systematic failures across sample difficulty, acoustic conditions, or subpopulations. We adapt this philosophy to cough distillation. Instead of only asking which model has the best mean AUROC, we ask where KD helps, where it fails, and whether target-unlabeled signals can predict these differences.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The novelty boundary is deliberately narrow. This paper does not claim first KD under shift, first respiratory KD, first cough representation distillation, first cough representation learner, first cross-dataset COVID cough classifier, or a new respiratory foundation model. The remaining contribution is a deployment audit: a multi-evidence failure map showing that aggregate AUROC, calibration, domain readability, target slices, bootstrap subsets, and evaluation units can disagree for ultra-light cough KD.
