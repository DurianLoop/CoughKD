\section{Introduction}

Cough and respiratory sounds have attracted substantial interest as low-cost signals for health screening. Public datasets such as COUGHVID and Coswara enabled rapid progress on COVID-era cough modeling \cite{orlandic2021coughvid,bhattacharya2023coswara}. However, direct clinical claims from cough audio remain fragile. Labels can be self-reported or noisy, recruitment channels can differ across classes, recording devices and environments can leak shortcuts, and symptoms or demographic variables can confound performance \cite{han2022sounds}. A model that performs well on one dataset may therefore provide weak evidence of deployable clinical utility.

This paper does not aim to prove that cough audio diagnoses COVID-19. Instead, we study a deployment reliability question: when large audio teachers are distilled into ultra-light cough students, does KD remain reliable under external dataset shift? This question matters because high-capacity teachers such as PANNs \cite{kong2020panns}, AST \cite{gong2021ast}, and BEATs \cite{chen2023beats} can provide useful audio representations, but their memory and latency profiles are poorly matched to always-on mobile or edge screening.

KD is often judged by aggregate accuracy or AUROC. For cough audio deployment, that is not enough. A compact model may improve calibration while hurting external ranking; a representation may become less dataset-readable without becoming more task-useful; a method may win at the clip level but not after aggregating clips to subjects. These mismatches are central because deployment decisions are made under changing target populations, devices, symptoms, recording quality, and evaluation units.

We introduce \textsc{CoughKD-ShiftAudit}, a failure-oriented audit protocol for ultra-light cough audio distillation. Rather than presenting a new diagnostic classifier or an unbounded architecture search, we audit a bounded set of KD assumptions under source-to-target shift. We train an ultra-light depthwise CNN student on Coswara cough audio and evaluate on two independent public external targets, COUGHVID and Tos COVID-19 \cite{buenosaires2021toscovid}, with Virufy used only as a small public stress target. The audit jointly reports external AUROC/AUPRC, calibration, high-specificity sensitivity, domain and task probes, checkpoint size, latency, metadata-defined target slices, bootstrap target-subset stability, overlap control, and clip-vs-subject stress tests.

Our current evidence reveals a central tension. The student is extremely deployable, with only 20.7K parameters and roughly 3912x fewer parameters than the PANNs CNN14 teacher. Yet the most stable KD variant improves COUGHVID external macro AUROC by only $+0.0007$ over source-only continuation. On Tos COVID-19, CE-only training is the post-hoc best method, vanilla KD is negative, and target-unlabeled guard selection also selects a negative-transfer variant. At the same time, Virufy stress targets show that post-hoc best methods and guard selections can change across target size, preprocessing granularity, and clip-vs-subject evaluation. This motivates an audit contribution rather than a premature method-superiority claim.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate ultra-light cough audio KD as a deployment reliability problem under dataset shift, avoiding unsupported clinical diagnostic claims.
    \item We propose \textsc{CoughKD-ShiftAudit}, an audit protocol combining external evaluation, calibration, probe-based representation evidence, slice stress tests, bootstrap target-subset tests, overlap control, evaluation-unit stress tests, and deployment metrics.
    \item We provide evidence that common and shortcut-aware KD variants yield weak, unstable, or negative external gains across Coswara-to-COUGHVID and Coswara-to-Tos transfer.
    \item We show that calibration, domain readability, target confidence, external AUROC, and clip-vs-subject aggregation can disagree, motivating multi-evidence reporting for cough audio deployment.
\end{itemize}
