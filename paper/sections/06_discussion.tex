\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What can be claimed.}
The current evidence supports \textsc{CoughKD-ShiftAudit} as a deployment-oriented audit protocol. Across COUGHVID and Tos COVID-19, ultra-light cough KD under dataset shift is method-sensitive and target-composition-sensitive; across Virufy stress targets, it is also evaluation-unit-sensitive. The strongest claim is not that a new KD loss wins, but that cough KD requires multi-evidence failure auditing before deployment.

\paragraph{What cannot be claimed.}
The current evidence does not support clinical COVID cough diagnosis utility, robust SOTA performance, or a new KD method that consistently beats baselines. It also does not establish that Virufy is a strong external validation cohort, because its effective independent sample size is small.

\paragraph{Why the negative result matters.}
Negative or weak KD gains are not a failure of the project if the audit is rigorous. They reveal that improvements in calibration, shortcut suppression, or target consistency can disagree with external ranking. This is precisely the kind of evidence needed before edge deployment claims are made.

\paragraph{Next evidence needed.}
For a stronger empirical scope, the next evidence should come from larger targets such as UK COVID-19 Vocal Audio or Cambridge COVID-19 Sounds / ComParE CCS. If DiCOVA is added, it must be reported as a Coswara-related auxiliary protocol unless overlap control proves otherwise. A non-COVID cough transfer target could broaden the claim to generic cough deployment reliability.
