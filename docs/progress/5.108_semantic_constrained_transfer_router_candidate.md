# 5.108 Semantic-constrained transfer router candidate

## Purpose

5.107 found that the target-calibrated transfer controller clears the relaxed 1-3 point mean-improvement target on COUGHVID and TosCOVID, but the selector is noisy and not yet claimable.

This note performs the next low-cost diagnostic and tests a new candidate:

> Semantic-Constrained Transfer Router: use target metadata semantics to restrict which transfer strategy is allowed. Symptom/acquisition slice metadata enables slice gating; demographic-only metadata routes to target-calibrated logistic stacking.

No model training or external downloads were run.

## Literature collision anchors

The novelty space is narrow. Existing work already covers:

- cross-dataset COVID cough transfer with cough detection, segmentation, and augmentation: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05843
- drift-adaptive COVID cough diagnosis and domain adaptation framing: https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e66919
- multi-center cough validation with abundant metadata and factor analysis: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1532046425000279
- symptom-assisted audio COVID detection: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1551298/pdf
- negative transfer as a known transfer-learning risk: https://www.ieee-jas.net/en/article/doi/10.1109/JAS.2022.106004

Therefore this candidate cannot claim novelty as "ensemble," "stacking," "metadata use," or "domain adaptation."

The only plausible claim shape is more specific:

> Under COVID cough dataset shift, target metadata should not be treated as uniformly transferable. A lightweight router constrains adaptation strategy by metadata semantics: symptom/acquisition slices can define deployment gates, while demographic-only slices are treated as unsafe for slice transfer and routed to calibration stacking.

This is still a hypothesis, not a confirmed novelty claim.

## Claim status

Not claimable yet.

Positive:

- Clears the relaxed 1-3 point mean target on both large local external targets.
- Improves over the previous nested controller by +0.77 points on COUGHVID and +1.66 points on TosCOVID.
- Has a simple mechanism tied to the observed failure mode: do not use demographic-only slices as transfer gates.
- Oracle-error audit supports the mechanism: the previous controller's main problem was wrong strategy family selection.

Remaining risks:

- The 2.5% split-delta quantile is still negative on both targets.
- The rule currently has only two large targets; it may be overfit to COUGHVID/TosCOVID metadata schemas.
- The novelty claim still needs a deeper literature collision audit against domain-adaptive ensembling, metadata-conditioned adaptation, and selective/guarded prediction.
- A strong ICASSP 2027 version likely still needs UKCOVID/Cambridge/HeAR external evidence.

## Next low-cost hardening experiments

1. Random semantic-label negative control:
   - randomly mark `age` as semantic and `symptom_resp` as nonsemantic;
   - the effect should collapse or reverse.
2. Metadata-column leave-one-rule audit:
   - compare semantic whitelist variants to test whether the gain is specifically symptom/acquisition semantics rather than target-name leakage.
3. Calibration-label budget:
   - reduce outer calibration fraction from 50% to 30%, 20%, and 10%;
   - a practical ICASSP claim should not require half of the target labels.
4. Third-target readiness:
   - UKCOVID/Cambridge/HeAR remains the right external validation route if the user approves access/download.

Current recommendation:

Continue local low-cost hardening with negative controls before spending more on model training or gated assets.
