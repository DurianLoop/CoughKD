Protocol:

1. Split each target into outer calibration and outer held-out evaluation.
2. Split outer calibration into inner train and inner validation.
3. Let inner validation select among source-only, calibration-best single model, logistic stacking, and a target metadata slice gate when available.
4. Refit the selected strategy on the full outer calibration split.
5. Evaluate only on the outer held-out split.

## Interpretation

Under the relaxed 1-3 point target, the target-calibrated transfer controller becomes the closest current candidate:

- COUGHVID mean improvement is +2.98 points.
- TosCOVID mean improvement is +2.33 points.
- The selector usually chooses the expected family: slice gate for COUGHVID and logistic stacking for TosCOVID.

However, this is still not a defensible ICASSP claim:

- both 95% confidence intervals include negative effects;
- `p(delta < 1pt)` is still high: 25.6% on COUGHVID and 38.8% on TosCOVID;
- TosCOVID strategy selection remains noisy;
- the method is close to standard stacking/model selection unless the novelty is framed and supported as target-calibrated negative-transfer routing;
- no independent third target confirms that the routing rule generalizes.

## Provisional novelty shape

The most plausible local-only claim shape is:

> Target-Calibrated Transfer Strategy Routing: a small target calibration set is used to route deployment among source-only inference, target-calibrated stacking, and metadata-slice transfer gates so that negative transfer is avoided under dataset shift.

Novelty status:

- Not confirmed as "previously undone."
- Needs a focused literature collision audit against target-domain model selection, domain-adaptive ensembling, cross-dataset cough diagnosis, and selective prediction under shift.
- The current empirical evidence is suggestive but not stable enough.

## Next low-cost experiment

Do not run another blind training grid yet.

The next experiment should be an oracle-error audit of the controller:

1. For each outer repeat, compute held-out performance for every available strategy, not only the selected one.
2. Compare the selected strategy to the held-out oracle best strategy.
3. Classify failures into:
   - wrong strategy family selected;
   - correct family selected but calibration refit underperforms;
   - all strategies weak on that split;
   - source-only would have avoided a negative transfer event.
4. Use this to design the next selector signal.

This is cheaper and more informative than another weight sweep. It directly tests whether the problem is selector reliability or strategy capacity.
