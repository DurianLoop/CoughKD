# 5.92 Symptom-Slice Confounding Audit

## Purpose

Audit a likely reviewer concern for the current best candidate:

> Is the margin-regularized `symptom_resp` gate merely exploiting symptom metadata as a proxy label?

The candidate should be a transfer gate, not a metadata-only classifier.

Interpretation:

Interpretation:

- Most gate gain comes from the smaller `symptom_resp=true` slice.
- That slice has higher COVID-positive and respiratory-illness prevalence.
- Candidate F is substantially better than source-only inside that slice.
- The `false` slice contributes little improvement.

## Risk Assessment

Positive:

- The gate is not merely a symptom-only classifier; symptom-only macro AUROC is lower than the gated model.
- The random-slice negative control from `5.91` showed that matched random binary splits do not reproduce the effect.
- The slice-specific model choice is interpretable: Candidate F helps where respiratory condition is present.

Risk:

- `symptom_resp` is correlated with the target labels.
- Reviewers may argue the method relies on target metadata that partially encodes disease state.
- The claim must not be framed as metadata-free cough diagnosis.

## Claim Boundary

Safer framing:

> Given target symptom metadata and moderate target calibration labels, a margin-regularized symptom-slice gate can reduce negative transfer by choosing which cough model to deploy within clinically meaningful subgroups.

Unsafe framing:

> A cough-only model improves COVID detection by 3-5 points.

## Decision

Keep the candidate alive, but require:

- explicit metadata-available setting,
- symptom-only baseline,
- random-slice negative control,
- cross-target validation, preferably UKCOVID.

Do not claim metadata-free acoustic improvement.
